# Show Bible v0.1 — proposal for owner review

**Status: PROPOSAL ONLY — NOT APPROVED CANON.** This document does not supersede the status of any existing show-bible file. The human owner alone can approve premise, audience, character identity, powers, world, comedy engine or episodes under `DEC-0005`. Research basis: [Sprint 001 synthesis](../03_research/RESEARCH_SPRINT_001_SYNTHESIS.md), [concept stress test](../03_research/PIP_NIMBUS_FEASIBILITY.md), [source register](../03_research/SOURCE_REGISTER.md). Every creative choice below is an **inference to test**, not a research finding about children.

## 1–5. Series frame

1. **Working premise:** In a small, familiar everyday setting, methodical Pip and eager Nimbus pursue a practical goal. Nimbus's limited weather-like help sometimes causes a surprising but reversible consequence; together they adapt. The location, species, look and exact powers remain undefined.
2. **Audience recommendation:** Prototype for 4–6, with approximately 3–7 accessibility; owner decision pending. The specific age preference remains untested. See [age evidence](../03_research/CHILD_DEVELOPMENT/AGE_4_6_EVIDENCE.md).
3. **Core viewer promise:** A clear little problem, a playful surprise, a kind repair and an ending that feels complete. No promise of formal learning efficacy.
4. **Entertainment philosophy:** Comedy and feeling lead. A child should understand the immediate goal on first viewing and be able to enjoy a second viewing without being pressed to continue.
5. **Educational philosophy:** Curiosity, cooperation, patience and observation may emerge from action. Real science can inspire practical observation; Nimbus's fantasy abilities must not be presented as real meteorology. No lecture or mandatory moral line.

## 6–11. Characters and world

6. **Pip, working definition:** A small, organized planner who notices patterns and cares about outcomes. Pip can be flexible and funny, not only rigid or correct. Species, pronouns, appearance and age are open.
7. **Nimbus, working definition:** A warm-hearted, cloud-like helper who acts before fully understanding a situation. Nimbus has agency and learns to listen; Nimbus is not a limitless device or a perpetual fool. Appearance, pronouns and identity are open.
8. **Relationship:** Cooperative friends with different methods. Neither is punished for being themselves; each contributes to resolution. Test whether the relationship reads without explanatory dialogue.
9. **World concept:** A compact, recurring environment with familiar objects and simple physical logic. Specific place, inhabitants and map await owner choice and originality review.
10. **Proposed power system:** For early testing only, mild rain or breeze as one visible input with a visible consequence. Snow, heat, lightning, shape change and size change stay excluded from initial tests, **not permanently decided**.
11. **Proposed limitations:** Each effect has a visible trigger, duration and cost or tradeoff; it cannot instantly solve the whole problem. No unannounced powers. Weather never creates realistic severe danger. All rules need child comprehension and production testing before canon.

## 12–21. Episode grammar

12. **Comedy engine:** A clear expectation followed by an original, benign incongruity, recognition pause, escalating consequence and earned repair. Rule of three is optional and unproven for this audience.
13. **Story engine:** Everyday goal → character-specific attempt → observable consequence → changed plan → joint action → complete ending. Some episodes should begin from Pip's choice, some from Nimbus's, some from a shared misunderstanding, to avoid a single template.
14. **Episode ritual:** A small recurring signal of readiness and a satisfying close may aid familiarity, but never a compulsory cliffhanger. Test whether the ritual adds comfort or feels repetitive.
15. **Escalation:** Grow one problem by cause and effect; no random pileup. Consequences remain reversible, emotionally bounded and safe. Recovery is visible on screen.
16. **Dialogue:** Visual-first with expressive sound and sparse, easily translated verbal anchors if needed for causal comprehension. Compare silent and anchored edits before locking. Research directly cautions against assuming video-only narrative is always easier for 5–6s [S04](https://doi.org/10.1177/009365088015003001).
17. **Visual storytelling:** Strong pose/silhouette contrast, clean object continuity, one focal action per shot and enough time to perceive cause. No approved palette, camera grammar or character art exists.
18. **Emotional framework:** Recognizable disappointment, jealousy, embarrassment or worry may appear only at gentle intensity, with empathy and repair. Humor targets the situation, never a person or group.
19. **Parent co-viewing:** Offer an honest adult-readable feeling or choice without adult-only jokes that distract from the child story. Leave natural discussion space; co-presence alone is not proven to improve comprehension [S15](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ804884), while AAP encourages active shared media use [S17](https://www.healthychildren.org/English/family-life/Media/Pages/why-co-viewing-is-important-tips-to-share-screen-time-with-your-kids.aspx).
20. **Internationalization:** Carry goal, action and feeling across languages; use short, translatable phrases only where helpful. Test in more than one language/culture; low dialogue does not guarantee universal visual understanding [S06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7093370/).
21. **Rewatch strategy:** First viewing works alone; later viewing may reveal a small acting detail or fair callback. No manipulative loops, withheld resolution or misleading thumbnails. Repeated exposure helps some comprehension but not every inference [S13](https://research.monash.edu/en/publications/do-young-children-get-the-message-the-effects-of-repeated-video-v/), [S14](https://doi.org/10.1080/03637750600693464).

## 22–25. Integrity and production

22. **Originality:** Use only abstract mechanics learned from reference works. Develop independent shapes, names, world grammar, staging, gags, outcomes and branding. A qualitative rights review must examine cloud/weather and helper-character similarities before design approval.
23. **Production constraints:** Reference images and first/last frames are documented capabilities, not reliable series continuity [S38](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo?hl=en), [S40](https://helpx.adobe.com/firefly/web/work-with-audio-and-video/work-with-video/generate-videos-using-images.html). Benchmark prop contact, repeated characters, weather effects and shot continuity. No renderer, service or stack is chosen.
24. **Safety/editorial constraints:** Apply `AGENTS.md`, `02_story_system/AGE_4_6_RULES.md`, child-safety checks and human approval gates. Any sensitive-content trigger receives `SENSITIVE_CONTENT_REVIEW_REQUIRED` and no automatic story generation. Keep weather mild and nonimitable. Made for Kids and YouTube Kids decisions happen at publication review [S20](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174?hl=en), [S26](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/2801999?hl=en-GB).
25. **Forbidden patterns:** Cruel humiliation, discriminatory humor, ideology or adult themes, unsafe imitation, frightening weather peril, magical instant fixes, generic template plots, manipulative repetition, hidden paid promotion, misleading science claims or metadata, and copied expressive sequences.

## 26–28. Open questions, risks and owner decisions

26. **Open creative questions:** What are Pip and Nimbus visually? Is Nimbus actually a cloud? What is the smallest coherent power set? What recurring world supports varied goals? How much speech improves comprehension? Can Pip initiate chaos and Nimbus solve a problem without powers? What would distinguish the property from existing cloud/weather IP?
27. **Risks:** Pure silence could obscure plot; weather makes physics and animation hard; broad powers weaken stakes; a single helper-error template becomes mass-produced; older viewers may want deeper humor; younger viewers may be frightened; merchandising and global demand have no supporting test. See [concept assessment](../03_research/PIP_NIMBUS_FEASIBILITY.md).
28. **Human decisions before Canon v1.0:** Core/elastic age, concept choice versus Fix-It/Lumina, character identities and count, premise/world, allowed powers and limits, comedy/story engine, dialogue degree, episode duration, pacing, educational intensity, co-viewing priority, visual direction, originality review criteria, pilot requirements and budget. The [decision matrix](../03_research/RESEARCH_SPRINT_001_SYNTHESIS.md#owner-decision-matrix) records alternatives and deferral costs. Owner approval must be explicit and logged as later decisions.

## Development examples — NOT APPROVED EPISODES

These **eight short premise stress tests** are not scripts, episode canon or a slate commitment. They deliberately vary emotional driver and causal mechanism; any resemblance to existing work requires review.

1. **Too much shade:** A gentle cloud shade helps a wilting plant, then blocks light from a second plant; Pip and Nimbus must find a balanced placement. Tests mild weather and observation.
2. **The shared ribbon:** A breeze carries one ribbon between two friends who both need it; a fair arrangement matters more than another gust. Tests sharing.
3. **Wait for the paint:** Nimbus tries to dry a painted sign with a breeze before it is ready, making a harmless smudge; patience and a repair turn the mark into a new shape. Tests patience and callback.
4. **A quiet hello:** Pip misreads Nimbus's gesture while preparing a welcome; the visual misunderstanding resolves when each copies the other's viewpoint. Tests nonverbal comprehension.
5. **The puddle path:** Mild rain creates stepping stones of dry ground in the wrong places; the pair rearrange their route without demanding that Nimbus erase the rain. Tests physical cause and problem solving.
6. **One leaf too many:** Nimbus gathers scattered leaves for Pip, then a small breeze keeps uncovering a hidden leaf; a final still moment lets them notice it. Tests anticipation and visual callback.
7. **The borrowed spot:** Pip's carefully chosen spot is occupied by a friend; Nimbus offers cloud cover, but the emotional answer is asking and cooperating. Tests friendship and limits of powers.
8. **Which way is cool?:** The pair compare sun and shade for a picnic object and revise their first guess from observation; Nimbus's fantasy role remains clearly separate from the real-world cause. Tests simple science boundary.
