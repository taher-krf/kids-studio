# Audience age evidence: four candidate cores

Status: RESEARCH / RECOMMENDATION ONLY. Accessed 2026-09-25. Source metadata and limits: [SOURCE_REGISTER](../SOURCE_REGISTER.md). Age bands overlap deliberately; this is a format decision, not a developmental diagnosis. No merch preference or exact video attention span is established by these sources.

## Research question and method

Which of 3–4, 4–6, 5–7, or 6–8 best fits warm, short, visual-first cause-and-effect comedy? Searched CDC developmental milestones, peer-reviewed television/visual narrative studies, AAP guidance, YouTube Kids age settings, and Ofcom audience reports. Accepted primary/official sources [S01](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/3-years.html), [S02](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/4-years.html), [S03](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/5-years.html), [S04](https://doi.org/10.1177/009365088015003001), [S05](https://doi.org/10.1207/S1532785XMEP0304_04), [S06](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7093370/), [S18](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/siteassets/resources/documents/research-and-data/media-literacy-research/children/2026-children-and-parents-report/children-and-parents-media-use-and-attitudes-report-2025-6.pdf?v=418231), [S19](https://www.ofcom.org.uk/media-use-and-attitudes/media-habits-children/a-window-into-young-childrens-online-worlds), [S20](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10938174?hl=en). Rejected unsourced attention-span rules, creator anecdotes as population evidence, and merchandising claims derived from channel popularity.

**Recommendation — MEDIUM / PRIMARY + INFERENCE:** Keep **4–6 as the working core** and **about 3–7 as an accessibility envelope**, because the format's simple causal and emotional material overlaps known 4- and 5-year milestones, while the UK audience data confirms animation is relevant among 3–5s. This is not proof of optimality. The strongest argument against it is [S04](https://doi.org/10.1177/009365088015003001): a no-dialogue strategy can reduce story comprehension at 5–6. A 5–7 core could outperform if prototype tests show children need longer causal chains or older social humor; a 3–4 core could outperform if the material becomes simpler and more music/ritual driven. Ask the owner after age-stratified comprehension and preference tests.

## Development features to test

Object permanence is established well before these candidate ages; it is not a useful differentiator for this audience choice. Symbolic/fantasy understanding is developing and context-sensitive: avoid assuming a weather effect is either believed literally or recognized as fiction by every child [S07](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jecp.2012.04.010). Test whether children can retell the goal, the help attempt, the unintended effect, and the repair; separately ask how they believe the effect happened. Test emotion reading, fear reaction, favorite gag, spontaneous desire to replay, and caregiver acceptability. No attention-span number from CDC should be applied to video: its 5–10-minute activity milestone explicitly says screen time does not count [S03](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/5-years.html).

## Remaining uncertainty

No local child panel, cross-cultural comprehension data, merch choice study, longitudinal rewatch study, or direct comparison of these four target segments was reviewed. Confidence in **specific segment superiority is LOW** even though development-stage descriptions are reasonably supported.
