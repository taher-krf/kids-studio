Status: **DEVELOPMENT / STRESS-TEST MATERIAL — NOT CANON. Bounded control, not a reopening of alternatives** (`DEC-0014` keeps both paused). Date: 2026-09-26 (Europe/Istanbul). Data: `data/raw/control_sample.jsonl` (10 premises each, same schema, same coding vocabularies). ## Questions the control was asked

1. Does Pip + Nimbus generate more mechanism diversity?
2. Is it substantially harder to produce?
3. Does its character engine feel more or less distinctive?

## Findings (directional, LOW/INFERENCE)

1. **Mechanism diversity:** at n=10 all three concepts show 100% distinct signatures — small samples cannot discriminate. The meaningful difference is at scale: Pip+Nimbus's diversity was demonstrated to 125 signatures; the controls were not tested at scale. What the control *does* show qualitatively: Fix-It's diversity runs on **problem taxonomy** (diagnose/repair/build variants) and Lumina's on **observation rituals** — both are real but narrower emotional bandwidths in this sample: no jealousy, embarrassment, or social-expectation premise arose naturally for either control without forcing it, whereas Structure A generated those families for Pip+Nimbus on demand.
2. **Production burden:** the clearest result. Fix-It runs on deterministic props (prop_contact high, but no vapor, no wet-state, minimal secondary motion); Lumina is even lighter. Pip+Nimbus is **structurally heavier** (vapor continuity + wet states + calibrated wind), and its quality peak sits in EXTREME-burden premises (ANALYSIS_REPORT §7). If the production benchmark prices vapor/wet continuity as badly as the red team fears, Fix-It wins on economics per accepted minute. This is exactly the red team's trigger B1 ("benchmark shows continuity yield so low that Fix-It's controllable-physics advantage is decisive") — still unmeasured, still the open gate.
3. **Character engine distinctiveness:** Fix-It's comedy is single-lead trait comedy (perfectionism/stubbornness against objects); Lumina's is gentle obsession against time. Neither produced a *relational* engine in this sample — the forecast-irony / leak-reading dyad has no equivalent in either control. Pip+Nimbus's engine is the most distinctive of the three **and** the most dependent on expensive acting.

**Control conclusion:** the alternatives are not shown to be stronger; Fix-It is shown to be **cheaper per accepted minute under plausible assumptions**. The choice between them is a production-economics question first and a creative question second. Recommendation: keep both paused; if the weighted production benchmark fails Pip+Nimbus on cost-per-accepted-second, Fix-It is the justified reopening candidate (not Lumina — its engine is thinner for a 4–6 comedy).
