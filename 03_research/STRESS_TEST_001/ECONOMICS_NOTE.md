## 1. The cost structure this concept chooses

From the accepted pool (n=142): LOW 4.2% / MEDIUM 58.5% / HIGH 30.3% / EXTREME 7.0%. Production flags concentrate exactly where generative tools are weakest: `wet_state` ≥ medium in ~45% of accepted premises, `subtle_acting` high in ~30%, `vapor_continuity` high in ~6%. Per accepted minute of the *best* material (the expressive-power peaks), the cost drivers are: vapor-body identity continuity, persistent wet/dry state, calibrated multi-object secondary wind motion, and reaction-acting holds. Every one of these is a documented generative weak point (S38–S40, S43); none is amortizable (each wet-state and vapor shot is bespoke; rigid props and sets are the reusable layer this concept has least of).

## 2. Hidden dependencies exposed by this test

1. **The quality peak and the cost peak are the same episodes** (ANALYSIS_REPORT §7). A slate cheapened by avoiding EXTREME premises is a slate stripped of its ownable content; a slate made of them is the most expensive option. Slate *mixing* is not optional; it is the economics.
2. **The alternative (Fix-It) is cheaper for structural reasons** (deterministic props, no weather states — CONTROL_SAMPLE.md). The Pip+Nimbus premium is not a yield-rate problem alone; it is a permanent content mix.
3. **Localization savings are real but small**: sparse dialogue cuts dubbing, but animation is the cost driver; the savings do not pay for the vapor/wet premium.
4. **Made-for-Kids structural constraints** (no personalized ads, disabled engagement features, no on-video merch shelf) cap the primary platform revenue path; watch-time economics push toward volume, and YPP inauthentic-content rules punish exactly the volume-with-template profile (S24). The premise-coding trail produced by this test (mechanism signatures, rejection records) is itself a compliance asset for that review.
5. **Non-ad IP paths** (books, licensing) are downstream of distinctive identity — which this phase shows is concentrated in the expensive expressive-power layer.

## 3. What is now measurable

The stress test converts the economics question from "can AI make children's animation cheap?" (unanswerable) into: **"what does one EXTREME-burden expressive-power shot cost, at accepted quality, versus one MEDIUM character-comedy shot?"** That is answerable by the weighted production benchmark (`PROP-0005` modification 6), and the accepted pool's burden mix gives the benchmark a concrete slate to price: e.g., a 10-episode slate with the pool's mix implies ~6 MEDIUM, ~3 HIGH, ~1 EXTREME episodes; the benchmark should price that mix explicitly.

## 4. Explicit non-claims

No revenue forecast, no cost figure, no yield measurement, no tool selection. The single quantitative dependency remains: cost per accepted second on the five red-team stressors, unmeasured until the benchmark.
